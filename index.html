Method, sources and caveats

The site

The footprint is the Union Pacific Canal Street Yard as mapped in OpenStreetMap (way 56246278), roughly acres running along the South Branch of the Chicago River from about 16th Street to Pershing Road. Amtrak has not published a parcel-level plan; when it does, the buffer can be re-drawn from that footprint instead.

The existing-site columns use Amtrak's current maintenance yard south of Union Station (the Chicago Car Yard, OpenStreetMap way 880427556, about acres between Roosevelt Road and 18th Street along the river). The same distance and inclusion rule are applied to it; it is not drawn on the map.

Distances are straight-line feet in a local planar projection centred on the yard. Buffers are measured from the yard boundary, not its centre.

Three inclusion rules

Any part within distance counts a whole tract as soon as its boundary comes within the set distance. Simple and inclusive, but it credits the cohort with residents who may live half a mile further away.

Weighted by land area counts each tract in proportion to the share of its area inside the band (sampled on a 30-foot grid). Standard practice in exposure studies; assumes people are spread evenly across the tract.

Weighted by residents counts each tract in proportion to the share of its 2020 Census population living in blocks whose centre is inside the band. Usually the most faithful of the three, because it ignores rail land, river and industrial parcels where nobody lives.

Data

American Community Survey 5-year estimates, , at the tract level; the city comparison uses the same tables for Chicago city (place 1714000). Sentinel values for suppressed estimates are treated as missing. Medians cannot be summed, so median household income and median year built are shown as household-weighted averages of tract medians and labelled as such.

CDC PLACES, 2025 release (BRFSS ), model-based crude prevalence among adults 18+. These are small-area estimates, not surveys of these specific residents, and are aggregated here by adult population. The city figure is the PLACES crude prevalence for Chicago.

Geography from Census TIGERweb (2020 tracts and blocks with 2020 counts). Basemap from OpenStreetMap contributors.

What this cannot tell you

EPA's EJScreen, the usual source for existing pollution burden and traffic proximity, was taken offline in 2025 and is not included. Health prevalence here describes the population's baseline vulnerability, not an effect of the yard.

ACS tract estimates carry wide margins of error; small differences from the city figure may not be meaningful. Where a cohort figure's margin is large relative to the estimate, treat it as indicative.
